# Batch 28D.4 — Exact conditional mine probabilities

`calculateVisibleProbabilities(rows, columns, mineCount, board, limits?)` shares the bounded search with `enumerateVisibleBoard`, retaining its 18-cell frontier cap and 100,000-node budget. Existing enumeration return values and call sites are unchanged. No application caller uses the new probability function yet.

The model gives equal weight to every full-board mine arrangement compatible with visible clues and the public total. For each feasible frontier assignment with k remaining mines among N outside cells, there are C(N,k) compatible layouts. For a specific outside square, C(N-1,k-1) of those layouts contain a mine there (zero when k=0). The search accumulates per-frontier-mine-count bins and weights them after successful completion, without enumerating outside permutations. BigInt arithmetic preserves exact integer counts even on large boards; the returned decimal strings are JSON-safe. Divide each square's `mineLayouts` by `totalLayouts` for its model-conditional risk. Compare numerators directly within the same result. Exact zero and equality to the denominator, not rounded percentages, identify certainty. Do not display rounded 0% or 100% for non-forced squares.

No inference from flags, hidden adjacency numbers, or post-loss boards. Incomplete, invalid, inconsistent and terminal searches expose no probabilities. No approximation is supplied when limits are exceeded. This is not a calibrated prediction of the game's generator: opening protection, flood visibility, generation constraints or other prior knowledge can alter actual probabilities. Do not grade decisions, claim unavoidable guessing, or offer probability advice before these assumptions and user-facing language are reviewed. The full-size blank-board calculation is a model result, not a first-click risk estimate.

Tests compare exact counts against independent enumeration of all full-board layouts and revealed subsets on 2x2, 2x3 and 3x3, including synthetic snapshots. 11,461 unique snapshots and 59,185 covered-square probabilities checked. A 3x4 example demonstrates why weighting matters: five feasible frontier assignments correspond to 11 complete layouts, and a particular square occurs in four of those layouts, not one fifth. Additional tests cover budgets, invalid and terminal boards, flag independence, large exact counts and conservation of mine count on an Expert-size board. Full repository and realistic replay performance testing remain pending.
